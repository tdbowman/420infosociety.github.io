# Module 3: Artificial Intelligence in Society 🤖

**Week 3 | The Technology of the Century Meets Everyone Else**

## Welcome

You have almost certainly used artificial intelligence today — to autocomplete a text, unlock your phone, or draft something you didn't feel like writing. This week we stop treating AI as background noise and put it at the center of the information society story. Our goal is not to decide whether AI is "good" or "bad," but to build a working understanding of what these systems actually are, what they can and cannot do, and how they are already reshaping security, creativity, and everyday human relationships.

### 🎯 What You'll Learn

- The difference between rule-based (symbolic) AI, machine learning, deep learning, and generative AI — and where systems like ChatGPT fit in that family tree
- Why large language models are simultaneously fluent and unreliable, and what "hallucination" really means
- How the same AI capabilities that create benefits also expand, cheapen, and sharpen malicious uses — the dual-use problem
- What the evidence says about the gap between how AI experts and the general public see this technology
- How AI is already changing human communication and creativity, in ways both measurable and easy to miss

### 🧠 Big Questions to Consider

- When someone says a system is "intelligent," which definition of intelligence is doing the work — and who benefits from that framing?
- If a capability can diagnose disease *and* automate phishing, who should decide how it gets released?
- Why do the people who build AI feel so much more optimistic about it than the people who live with it?
- If an AI helped write a message to you, would you want to know? Would knowing change how you feel about the sender?
- Does a tool that makes each of us more creative individually make all of us more alike collectively?

## This Week's Journey

We start with the machinery: what AI is, how it learned to talk, and why the "is it really intelligent?" debate matters less than you might think. Then we widen the lens to society, using a famous 2018 forecast of AI misuse and fresh survey data on how Americans actually feel about the technology. Finally, we zoom all the way in — to your text messages and your creative writing — to see what happens when AI quietly joins your relationships.

### 📚 Core Readings

| Reading | Source | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| [The Malicious Use of Artificial Intelligence (Ch. 1–2)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.07228) | Brundage, M., et al. (2018) | The classic dual-use forecast: how AI expands, cheapens, and targets digital, physical, and political attacks |
| [Artificial intelligence in communication impacts language and social relationships](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-023-30938-9) | Hohenstein, J., et al. (2023), *Scientific Reports* | The smart-reply study: AI suggestions make conversations more positive — until people suspect you used them |
| [How the U.S. Public and AI Experts View Artificial Intelligence](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2025/04/03/how-the-us-public-and-ai-experts-view-artificial-intelligence/) | Pew Research Center (2025) | Documents the large optimism gap between AI experts and the American public |
| [Generative AI enhances individual creativity but reduces the collective diversity of novel content](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adn5290) | Doshi, A. R., & Hauser, O. P. (2024), *Science Advances* | An experiment showing AI helps individual writers while making everyone's stories more similar |
