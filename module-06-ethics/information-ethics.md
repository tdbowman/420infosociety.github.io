# Module 6: Information Ethics ⚖️

**Week 6 | From Principles to Power**

## Welcome

For five weeks you've been mapping the information society. This week we stop describing and start judging: when an algorithm screens your job application, when a chatbot becomes someone's closest confidant, when a government bans an AI system outright — who decided that was acceptable, by what standard, and who gets to enforce it? Information ethics is where the course's recurring question — *who benefits?* — becomes a discipline.

### 🎯 What You'll Learn

- Five ethical frameworks — consequentialism, deontology, virtue ethics, care ethics, and justice as fairness — and how to apply them as working tools, not vocabulary words
- The map of AI's classic ethical problems (opacity, bias, manipulation, responsibility gaps) and the new ones generative AI added
- Why researchers like Sasha Luccioni argue that present-day, measurable harms deserve more attention than speculative doomsday scenarios
- How the world is trying to turn ethical principles into actual rules: UNESCO's global recommendation, the EU's risk-tiered AI Act, and America's lighter touch
- How to run a structured ethical analysis of a real case — the core skill of Case Analysis Track A

### 🧠 Big Questions to Consider

- If five reasonable ethical frameworks give five different verdicts on the same system, is that a bug or a feature?
- Is an AI harm you can measure today more important than a catastrophe someone can only imagine?
- Can a document signed by 193 countries change anything if it isn't binding on any of them?
- Should an AI companion that makes a lonely person feel better be celebrated, regulated, or banned?
- Do ethics principles without enforcement change corporate behavior — or just corporate websites?

## This Week's Journey

We move from tools, to problems, to power. First, you'll assemble an ethical toolkit: five frameworks, each tested against the same hiring algorithm so you can watch them produce genuinely different verdicts. Second, you'll tour the issue map of AI ethics — the classic problems Müller catalogs and the generative-AI problems Hagendorff adds. Third, you'll follow ethics on its journey toward law, from UNESCO's principles to the EU's enforceable risk tiers to Washington's executive orders — and ask whether principles without teeth do anything at all.

:::{note} Before Class
Start with the Luccioni TED talk (it's short and it will sharpen your instincts), then read the Values and Principles sections of the UNESCO Recommendation — just those sections. Use Müller as a reference: the introduction, plus two problem areas that interest you. Read Bernardi in full; it's the case everything this week converges on. Throughout, keep one AI system you actually use in mind and ask of each reading: what would this author say about it?
:::

### 📚 Core Readings

| Reading | Source | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| [Ethics of artificial intelligence and robotics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-ai/) | Müller, V. C. (2026). *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*. | Open access; substantively revised March 2026. The field's reference map: privacy, manipulation, opacity, bias, autonomy, machine ethics, and the superintelligence debates. Skim strategically. |
| [Recommendation on the Ethics of AI](https://unesdoc.unesco.org/ark:/48223/pf0000381137) | UNESCO. (2021). | Open access. The first global standard-setting instrument on AI ethics, adopted by 193 member states. Read the Values and Principles sections. |
| [Mapping the ethics of generative AI: A comprehensive scoping review](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11023-024-09694-w) | Hagendorff, T. (2024). *Minds and Machines, 34*, 39. | Open access. Taxonomizes 19 ethical issue areas specific to generative AI — hallucination, alignment, impersonation, labor, and more. |
| [EU AI Act: First regulation on artificial intelligence](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/topics/en/article/20230601STO93804/eu-ai-act-first-regulation-on-artificial-intelligence) | European Parliament. (2025). | Open access. A plain-language explainer of the Act's risk-tier framework, with provisions phasing in through 2025–2027. |
| [Friends for sale: The rise and risks of AI companions](https://www.adalovelaceinstitute.org/blog/ai-companions/) | Bernardi, J. (2025). Ada Lovelace Institute. | Open access. Companion chatbots, emotional dependency, sycophancy, and "echo chambers of validation" — this week's collision case. |
