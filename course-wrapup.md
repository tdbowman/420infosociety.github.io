# Course Wrap-Up 🎓

Fourteen modules ago, this book opened with one question: *what happens when a society runs on information?* You now know the honest answer: many things at once, few of them simple, none of them finished.

---

## What You Built This Semester

Strip away the topics and this course taught a small set of durable moves:

1. **Interrogate definitions.** "Information," "intelligence," "trust," "misinformation": every big argument you met this semester turned on which definition was in play, and on who benefited from that choice.
2. **Demand rulers for narratives.** From AI 2027 to automation panic to platform press releases, you learned to pair every story with a measurement: an AI Index, a DBIR, a payroll study, a field experiment.
3. **Run the loop both ways.** Technology shapes society; society shapes technology back through law, ethics, markets, norms, and refusal. Anyone who tells you only half of that loop is selling something.
4. **Hold tensions honestly.** Entry-level jobs are being reshaped *and* there's no mass unemployment. AI companions comfort people *and* create dependencies. Community notes work *and* platforms adopted them partly for other reasons. Calibration beats certainty.
5. **Write, get reviewed, revise.** Two workshops and a revision memo taught the professional version of thinking: in public, with feedback, willing to change.

## Where the Story Goes Next

This book was revised in the summer of 2026, and parts of it will be out of date embarrassingly fast. Given the subject matter, that was always going to happen. The frameworks travel, though. When the next model generation, the next breach, the next platform pivot, or the next labor-market study arrives, you know what to ask: What's being measured? Who benefits? What would change my mind?
