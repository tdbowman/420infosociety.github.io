# Module 5: Cybersecurity in the Information Society 🛡️

**Week 5 | When the Information Society Gets Attacked**

## Welcome

For four weeks you've studied how the information society was built. This week we ask what happens when someone attacks it. Not "someone hacked a website" attacks, but attacks that empty pharmacy shelves, silence hospital billing systems, and sit quietly inside the phone network you used this morning.

### 🎯 What You'll Learn

- Why cybersecurity is a *societal* problem, involving hospitals, pipelines, and telecoms, rather than just an IT department's headache
- A working vocabulary of the threat landscape (ransomware, phishing, vulnerability exploitation, social engineering), grounded in the evidence of the 2026 Verizon Data Breach Investigations Report
- How the Change Healthcare breach unfolded, step by step, and what it reveals about single points of failure in critical infrastructure
- How national cybersecurity strategy changes when administrations change, and why that makes cybersecurity a political question as much as an engineering one
- How AI is transforming both attack and defense, including the first publicly reported AI-orchestrated espionage campaign

### 🧠 Big Questions to Consider

- If one company's breach can disrupt pharmacies nationwide, who was really attacked: the company, or the society?
- Is paying a ransom to criminals ever the responsible choice?
- Why do so many catastrophic breaches begin with something as mundane as a stolen password?
- When a national cyber strategy is rewritten every few years, can defenders plan for the long term?
- What changes when the "hacker" executing an intrusion is an AI agent rather than a person?

## This Week's Plan

We move from the wide shot to the close-up and back out. First, the threat picture: who attacks the information society, how, and how often, with real numbers instead of movie imagery. Second, the anatomy of one breach, Change Healthcare in February 2024, traced from a stolen credential to a nationwide healthcare outage. Third, defense: technical layers, national strategy, and the AI arms race running on both sides of the wall.
