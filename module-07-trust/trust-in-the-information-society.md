# Module 7: Trust in the Information Society 🤝

**Week 7 | The Invisible Infrastructure**

## Welcome

Last week you watched machines step into some of our most human relationships. This week we zoom out to the quiet force that makes the whole information society run: trust. You never see it, yet every login, purchase, ride-share, and chatbot conversation is a small act of faith in strangers, institutions, and code. That faith is currently being renegotiated on two fronts at once.

### 🎯 What You'll Learn

- Why trust is the invisible infrastructure of digital life, presumed by every transaction and noticed only when it fails
- Riegelsberger, Sasse, and McCarthy's classic framework: the contextual and intrinsic properties that make someone worth trusting, and the signals that carry them
- The difference between *trust* (something you give) and *trustworthiness* (something the other party has, or fakes)
- How trusting a machine differs from trusting a person: cognitive versus emotional trust, and why over-trust and under-trust are both dangerous
- What current evidence says about the two-front squeeze: eroding confidence in institutions alongside growing emotional reliance on AI

### 🧠 Big Questions to Consider

- How many strangers, companies, and algorithms did you trust before breakfast today, and what were you actually trusting them to do?
- Can a machine be *trustworthy*, or only *trusted*? Does the difference matter?
- Is it more dangerous to trust AI too much or too little?
- If people trust their chatbot more than their government, what happens to the institutions that trust used to hold up?
- Who benefits when trust signals (badges, reviews, a warm conversational tone) can be manufactured at scale?

## This Week's Plan

We move in three steps. First, we take trust apart to see how it works: what has to be true, and what has to be *signaled*, before a rational person hands money, data, or vulnerability to a stranger. Second, we ask what changes when the stranger is a machine, and what recent research says about how people actually calibrate their trust in AI. Third, we confront the squeeze: institutional trust turning inward while emotional bonds with machines deepen, and what rebuilding well-placed trust could look like.

:::{note} Before Class
Read Riegelsberger, Sasse, and McCarthy first and slowly; that paper is the backbone of the whole module. Then read Glikson and Woolley, and skim Afroogh and Gillespie for their big findings rather than every detail. Fang et al. is short and striking; read it last. As you go, keep a running list of every trust decision you make in one ordinary day. You will be surprised how long it gets.
:::
