# About This Book 📘

This open online textbook was created for **INF 420: The Information Society** at Dominican University's School of Information Studies, and revised in Summer 2026 for the Fall 2026 offering. Happy days.

---

## Why an Open Textbook?

Three reasons:

1. **Cost**: You pay nothing. Every required reading in this course is either openly licensed, freely available online, or provided through Canvas. A course about the information society should practice what it preaches about information access.
2. **Currency**: The topics in this course — artificial intelligence, cybersecurity, misinformation — move faster than any printed textbook can. An online book can be updated every semester, and this one is.
3. **Honesty about sources**: Every chapter tells you where its ideas come from and links you to the original research. You'll never be more than one click from the primary source, which is exactly the habit this course is trying to build in you.

## How It Was Built

The book is written in [MyST Markdown](https://mystmd.org/) and built with [Jupyter Book](https://jupyterbook.org), an open-source publishing system used by universities and research projects around the world. The source files live on [GitHub](https://github.com/tdbowman/420infosociety.github.io), and the site is rebuilt automatically whenever the source changes.

Portions of the drafting and updating process were assisted by AI tools, with all content reviewed, curated, and approved by Dr. Bowman — a workflow we'll discuss openly in class, because using AI well (and disclosing it honestly) is one of the skills this course teaches.

## A Living Document

If you spot an error, a dead link, or an explanation that could be clearer, tell Dr. Bowman — catching problems in information systems is an informatics skill, and yes, it counts toward engagement.

## Relationship to Canvas

This book is your **content home**: concepts, readings, and context. **Canvas** is your **logistics home**: submission links, exact due dates, rubrics, and grades. When the two disagree about logistics, Canvas wins.
